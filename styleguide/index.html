The brand was fine. The rules were missing.

One measurement explains this entire system. The primary call-to-action on avocadots.com — the button on the conversion path — sat at less than half the legibility the standard requires.

The green was never the problem. The problem was that one colour was doing two jobs. As a fill it is excellent. As text it is illegible. Splitting those roles removes the trap across all 180+ pages without altering the brand.

Scope note. These are measurements of the colour pairing, which nothing in the site's styling prevented. An earlier version of this page also claimed the failure sat on specific live buttons — that was inferred rather than verified, and is withdrawn. Foundations

Colour

Deep forest #1c3830 is the darkest shade of the brand green #86bd42. The two colours Avocadots already owned are the two ends of one ramp.

Green

Fill and text are different roles. --brand (#86bd42) is a fill — buttons, blocks, accents. --brand-text (#436421) is for green text and links. Never swap them.

The fill lifts on hover, it does not darken. The label on it is forest ink, so a darker fill would drop the pair to 4.15:1. Lifting to green-300 holds 7.09:1.

Gold — the second brand colour

Corrected. This originally read “the single warm accent… not a second brand colour”. That was wrong about this brand. On /web-design gold carries the primary CTA, the nav CTA, an entire section background, and a highlighter on running copy — four structural roles. Green is the identity; gold is what asks for the click.

Gold is unusually restrictive. On #eac435: forest is 7.51:1 and works; green-800 is 5.68:1 and works; but --ink-muted is only 3.31:1, --brand-text is 4.04:1, green-400 is 1.33:1, and white is 1.69:1 — all fail. On a gold surface nearly everything resolves to forest, and hierarchy comes from size and weight rather than a lighter ink. The .on-gold context encodes this, including inverting the CTA to a forest fill.

Neutrals

One ramp with a faint green undertone, so greys sit with the brand instead of fighting it. Replaces #eef0f1, #f0f0f0, #8f8f8f and #6e6e6e.

The category palette

A separate job from the brand palette: telling things apart. Team avatars, service markers, tag identity — where a reader needs to distinguish eight things at a glance and the brand ramp alone cannot. Eight tones at consistent chroma; forest and moss are the brand's own, so the family is anchored rather than bolted on.

Identity, never state. A category colour says which one — never how it is going. That is what --success, --warning and --danger are for. A berry avatar must never imply a problem.

Assign once and keep it. When a person or service owns a tone it is theirs across the site. A palette that reshuffles is decoration, not a system. And the label colour is fixed — white on deep, forest on tint; both are verified against all eight.

Foundations

Typography

The pairing stays: Manrope for display and headings, Poppins for body and interface. What was missing was a scale — nine sizes with no tracking rules and no limit on line length.

Tracking tightens as size grows. Large type at default tracking reads as loose; small type set tight becomes unreadable. The scale encodes this — don't override it.

Body copy is capped at 68 characters (--measure), and only one --text-display appears per page.

Foundations

Space and radius

4pt base, 8pt rhythm. The value of a spacing scale isn't that 24px beats 25px — it's that when everything is on the scale, inconsistency becomes visible instead of invisible.

Radius — four values, down from seven

Nesting rule. An inner radius is smaller than its container's. A --radius-md image inside a --radius-lg card looks right; the same radius on both looks like a mistake.

Foundations

Elevation

The old site had none. Four levels now, tinted with forest rather than black — black shadows under a warm green palette read as grime, not depth.

Border first. On light surfaces prefer a hairline border to a shadow. A border defines an edge honestly; a shadow claims the element is floating, which is usually a lie. The good pattern is border at rest, shadow on hover.

On the forest canvas, shadows stop working. Levels 1 and 2 resolve to none; separation comes from --surface-raised instead.

Foundations

Motion

Motion should explain a change, not decorate it. If an animation doesn't help someone understand what just happened, remove it.

TokenValueUse
--duration-instant80msColour-only changes: hover tint, focus ring
--duration-fast140msButtons, tags, icon flips
--duration-base220msThe default — cards, hovers, expanding panels
--duration-slow360msMenus, drawers, accordions
--duration-entrance520msOnce-per-element scroll entrances

Don't

Animate height, top or margin. Each forces layout every frame and janks on mid-range phones.

Do

Animate transform and opacity. For an accordion, transition grid-template-rows from 0fr to 1fr.

Don't

Re-run entrances every time an element scrolls back into view, or stagger a list of twenty items.

Do

Trigger once, then unobserve. Keep hover lifts at 2px and entrance travel under 24px.

Every duration token collapses to 0ms under prefers-reduced-motion, so anything built on the tokens is already compliant.

Testimonial · forest

Never fabricate a number. Where a project has no measured outcome, use an honest qualitative one — "the market now updates its own trader list weekly, without us" — and leave the stat row out. A made-up figure is a liability, not a case study.

The reason these pages have no numbers is almost certainly that nobody captured a baseline. Add "what will we measure, and what is it today?" to project kick-off.

Stat values use tabular-nums so a row of figures aligns, and every label names its measurement window. A percentage without a timeframe is not evidence.

Inputs and disclosure

Field · light

Accordion · forest

How much does a website cost?

Answer in the first sentence, then elaborate. An FAQ that dodges the pricing question loses trust rather than protecting margin.

Do you work with startups?

Multiple panels can be open at once — auto-closing the previous answer is comparison-hostile on an FAQ.

Every field has a visible label. Placeholder text is not a label — it disappears the moment someone types, so anyone interrupted mid-form loses the question. Input font size stays at 16px minimum, or iOS Safari zooms on focus.

The case-study template

Every case study says what was built. None says what changed. The template makes the outcome mandatory.

The template

  1. Hero

    Headline, one-line promise, 3–4 proof points, primary CTA. At least one proof point is a number.

  2. The problem

    Name the reader’s suspicion before pitching — “Most Websites Look Fine. Most Websites Don’t Perform.” No CTA. This is the section most often skipped and the one that does the most work.

  3. Positioning

    A two-beat antithesis — “Design attracts attention. Performance earns trust.” — plus the client-side benefit list.

  4. The work

    Six or more project cards, each labelled by sector so a prospect can find themselves in the list.

  5. What you get

    Four outcome-headlined cards with the mechanism sub-listed underneath.

  6. Mid-page CTA

    Named-human booking. Four words beat six.

  7. FAQ

    Five to seven buyer objections. Keyword questions belong in the blog, where the site already has 74 posts.

  8. Contact form, cross-sell, CTA band

    One adjacent service, then the closing band.

The reference page has defects too. /web-design carries 18 <h1> elements where there should be one — a real accessibility and SEO fault, worse than the page it is beating. It also has four stray “Add” defaults and three copy errors: “Certified WIx Partner”, “Higher Convertsion Rates”, and “Built To be Found” against “Built to be Found” in the same component.

Practice

Applying it in Wix Studio

The site stays on Wix. There is no API for editor-side design settings, so the colour and type layers are entered by hand — which is exactly why the mapping is written down.

Twenty of Wix's twenty-five Site Colour slots are used; five are held for growth. The first six keep Wix's built-in roles, so Wix's own components inherit the right colours automatically.

SlotRename toValueWix's role

Rollout order

  1. Site Colours and Text Themes.

    Highest leverage — this alone fixes the contrast failure across all 180+ pages. Do the links-and-actions slot first.

  2. Header and footer.

    Seen on every page. Resolve the double header CTA while you're there.

  3. Templates, not pages.

    The case-study and listing layouts are CMS-driven — fixing a template fixes every page built on it. This is where "Tag One" dies.

  4. The placeholder sweep.

    Search all 62 case studies for "Add paragraph text". Hours of work, and it stops the portfolio contradicting the pitch.

  5. High-traffic pages, then the long tail.

    Home, Services, Projects, Contact, About — then the remaining service pages and posts.
