# Voice and calls to action

## The voice that's already there

The site's best copy is direct, confident and slightly blunt:

> "Growth isn't luck. It's strategy and creativity."
> "Not services. Not silos. One engine."
> "Projects that speak for themselves."
> "Most businesses don't fail from bad ideas — they fail from unclear brands."

Short sentences. A claim, then the reason. No hedging, no jargon. That is the
voice — this document just makes it repeatable.

The weaker copy on the site drifts into agency-standard abstraction:

> "With expertise, ambition, and a heart full of ideas, we do what we do best:
> work together to refine a solution that ultimately works and is fantastic."

Nothing in that sentence is false, and nothing in it is information. Any agency
could have written it about any project.

## Rules

**Lead with the claim.** Put the point in the first sentence. Someone scanning
reads the first sentence of each block and nothing else.

**Be specific or say nothing.** "150+ companies" is specific. "Fantastic
solutions" is not. If a sentence would be equally true of any competitor, cut it.

**Second person.** "Your brand tells your story" — the site already does this well.
Keep it. Talk about the reader more than about yourselves.

**Sentence case for headings.** Not Title Case. Only `--text-xs` eyebrows are
uppercase.

**Contractions are fine.** "Isn't", "we've", "you'll". They're how the good lines
already read.

**Cut the intensifiers.** "Truly", "really", "incredibly", "absolutely". They
weaken the sentence they're meant to strengthen.

**One idea per paragraph**, 2–4 sentences. Body copy is capped at 68ch, so a long
paragraph becomes a wall.

**British English** — "optimisation", "personalised", "colour". The site is
Cyprus-based and already uses it; be consistent, including in tag names and UI
labels.

## Calls to action

The site currently runs five competing labels — "Work with us", "Start Now",
"View Service", "Schedule a Meeting", "View All" — plus stray links labelled
**"Add"** on the homepage project and service lists, which are unedited component
defaults and should simply be deleted.

### The approved set

| Label | Use | Level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Start a project** | The main conversion action, site-wide | Primary |
| **Book a call** | Calendly / scheduling links | Primary |
| **See our work** | To the portfolio | Secondary |
| **View service** | Service card to service page | Secondary |
| **Read the case study** | Project card to case study | Secondary |
| **Read the article** | Blog card to post | Secondary |
| **View all projects** | Index links | Tertiary |
| **Get in touch** | Footer, low-commitment contexts | Tertiary |

Sentence case. Verb first. No exclamation marks.

### Rules

**One primary CTA per section.** The homepage hero currently pairs "Work with us"
with "Schedule a Meeting" as equals. Make one primary and one secondary — they ask
for very different levels of commitment.

**Match the label to the destination.** "Start a project" must land on the contact
form, not a service page. Mismatched CTAs are the fastest way to lose trust.

**Never "Click here", "Learn more", or "Submit".** "Learn more" is the most common
CTA on the web and says nothing about what is on the other side. And link text has
to make sense out of context — a screen-reader user listing links hears eight
identical "Learn more"s.

**Never "Add".** It isn't a call to action; it's a leftover default.

**"Start Now" is on probation.** It appears **ten times** on `/digital-marketing`
alone. It names no destination and no commitment level. Prefer "Start a project"
for the conversion action, or a named-human booking — "Book a call with Andreas"
— which outperforms "Book a Free Digital Marketing Consultation" on both length
and warmth. See `service-page-pattern.md`.

## Headline patterns that work here

| Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Claim, then reason | "Growth isn't luck. It's strategy and creativity." |
| Negation, then answer | "Not services. Not silos. One engine." |
| Outcome as promise | "Let's build a digital presence that does more than look good." |
| The cost of inaction | "Most businesses don't fail from bad ideas — they fail from unclear brands." |

All four are already on the site. Use them; don't invent a fifth style per page.

## Words to avoid

`solutions` · `synergy` · `leverage` (as a verb) · `cutting-edge` · `best-in-class`
· `passionate` · `game-changing` · `seamless` · `robust` · `holistic` ·
`we do what we do best`

Most are placeholders for a specific claim nobody made yet. When one appears, ask
what it was standing in for and write that instead.

## Numbers

- Numerals for anything countable: "3 metrics", "62 projects", "150+ companies".
- Always attach the window: "38% more enquiries **in the first 90 days**". A
  percentage with no timeframe is not evidence.
- Never round up into a claim you can't source. See
  `case-study-template.md` — the fallback for an unmeasured project is an honest
  qualitative outcome, not an invented number.
